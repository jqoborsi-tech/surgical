The Split of Work

You handle the practice. I handle the surgery.

Hosting is deliberately zero-lift: if your office can schedule a hygiene day, it can schedule a surgery day. There is no surgeon to hire and no surgical equipment to buy.

Your team handles

  • Scheduling the surgical day from your existing patient base
  • Insurance verification and collections — production runs through your office
  • One assistant to join me for the day
  • An operatory and your CBCT imaging

I bring and handle

  • Every case reviewed in advance from your CBCT and scans
  • The full surgical setup: instruments, implant motor, handpieces and burs, and PRF
  • Implants and consumables ordered and cost-shared per our agreement
  • Oral sedation as the standard tier; IV moderate sedation (CA permit) with full monitoring where your patients elect it
  • All post-op coverage: every patient gets my direct line, 24/7
  • My own malpractice coverage, as a 1099 surgical partner

Common Questions

What owners ask on the fit call

Are you replacing our oral surgeon?

No. I keep the predictable ~90% of surgical cases in your office and keep referring the unpredictable 10% to your oral surgeon, exactly as you do today. That case-selection discipline — every case approved in advance from your CBCT — is why surgery days run safely and on schedule.

How is the economics structured?

A straightforward split of surgical production, paid as compensation for the surgical services I perform — papered with a one-page 1099 agreement. Your office keeps 100% of the restorative. Materials are shared at cost. There are no fees, minimums, or costs to host.

Is our office equipped for surgery and sedation?

If you have an operatory and suction, yes. I bring the surgical kits, implants, PRF centrifuge, and the full sedation setup — oral sedation as standard, IV where elected — monitors, emergency kit, and protocols meeting Dental Board of California standards. Case selection is conservative and screened in advance from your CBCT.

Who manages complications and post-op calls?

I do. Every surgical patient leaves with written post-op instructions and my direct line. Your front desk never fields a complication alone, and your doctors get a restorative hand-off note on every case.

How do our doctors and staff benefit clinically?

Your assistants learn surgical set-ups, and your doctors get chairside insight into implant planning and the restorative sequence on their own patients. Many partner offices treat the surgery day as in-house CE.

What does the first day look like?

We start with a half-day pilot: 3-4 pre-screened cases from your existing charts. Your team sees the workflow, your patients stay in your chair, and you see the production number that walked in instead of out. Then we set a recurring day.

Recurring surgery days are open on my calendar — including Saturdays.

Mondays, Fridays, and Saturdays, Orange County / LA / Inland Empire. A 15-minute call tells us both whether your referral volume supports a day — and what that day would produce.
